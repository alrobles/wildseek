# WildSeek — 3-minute demo script

## Act 1 — the problem (0:00–0:30)
- Screen: map with HPAI detections layer on. 9,479 wild-bird positives.
- VO: "Highly pathogenic avian influenza moves along flyways. CDC and APHIS see the detections — but for the poultry supply, the question is *where can the next introduction happen?* That's a wildlife susceptibility question, not a surveillance one."

## Act 2 — what we built tonight (0:30–1:30)
- Screen: species picker → suitability layer fades in on a bird species.
- VO: "Tonight, we ran 101,376 ellipsoid niche fits — 88 candidates for each of 1,148 North American species — against 30-year ERA5 climate normals, with each species' accessible area built from its own occupied ecoregions. Every fit got an AIC table, 100% were accepted, and the winner predicts this."
- Switch species a couple of times; the map repaints per species.

## Act 3 — the agentic layer (1:30–2:15)
- Screen: decisions delta or briefing panel.
- VO: "An agent watches the campaign — not a cron job. A strict-JSON triage model reads each species' diagnostics, but only the ambiguous tail ever reaches it: near-tied AIC, sparse occurrences, marine ranges. Just 173 fits qualified — and 49 came back `flag_review`, each with a reason. It can drop, flag, or re-fit, and every verdict lands in an auditable delta. The nightly briefing on screen was generated by a second model over tonight's artifacts — baked to JSON, so this demo runs keyless."
- Show the delta table: species · verdict · why · AIC old→new.

## Act 4 — the product answer (2:15–3:00)
- Screen: risk layer = detection recency × mean bird suitability, aggregated to HUC8 — named watersheds ranked.
- VO: "Detections alone tell you where the virus *was*. Multiply a recency kernel over those 9,479 detections by mean bird suitability, and you get risk at 1-kilometre resolution — finer than county — ranked across 2,247 named HUC8 watersheds. At the top tonight: Piscataqua-Salmon Falls, Pamlico Sound, and Cedar-Portage. Actionable by name, not by coordinate. That's WildSeek — live now at wildseek.ecoseek.org, built tonight on Meta's models, served as one process on the EcoSeek stack."
